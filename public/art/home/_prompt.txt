
# CLASSIC RADIO VAULT

## SHOW CARD ARTWORK — MASTER PRODUCTION PROMPT

**Version 2.0 — October 2026**

### Objective

Create premium, historically evocative promotional collector-card artwork for **[RADIO SHOW TITLE]**, a classic American radio program.

Use the supplied *Jack Benny Program* collector card as the primary visual reference for illustration quality, professional craftsmanship, authentic vintage printing characteristics, composition, and distressed-paper treatment.

The finished artwork must resemble an authentic, beautifully preserved illustrated radio magazine advertisement or promotional collector card from approximately 1935–1955.

Each image must possess its own distinct identity while remaining unmistakably part of the **Classic Radio Vault** collection.

---

## 1. Mandatory Historical Research

**Research each radio program before generating its artwork.**

Do not rely exclusively on general familiarity with the program or assumptions about its historical appearance.

Establish the following:

1. The official radio program title and historically recognized title variations.
2. The earliest verified original radio broadcast year.
3. The latest verified original radio broadcast year.
4. The principal performers and characters most strongly associated with the program.
5. Historical promotional advertisements, magazine illustrations, publicity photographs, and original title treatments.
6. The show's principal genre, setting, atmosphere, and recognizable visual characteristics.
7. Period-appropriate costumes, hairstyles, props, equipment, architecture, and other relevant details.

### Research priorities

Use historically credible sources, including:

- Original radio program advertisements and promotional materials.
- Period radio magazines and newspaper advertising.
- Original publicity photography and studio portraits.
- Broadcast network archives and historical radio reference works.
- Established old-time-radio research collections.
- Cross-referenced historical broadcast schedules and programographies.

When the historical evidence conflicts, prioritize primary contemporary sources and corroborated broadcast records.

### Historical fidelity

Represent the performers as they appeared during the radio program's original broadcast era, rather than at unrelated stages of their careers.

Maintain recognizable facial characteristics and historically appropriate wardrobe.

Original promotional artwork should inform the visual identity of the new card without requiring an exact reproduction of an existing advertisement.

**Historical research is a mandatory prerequisite to artwork generation.**

---

## 2. Required Image Dimensions

- **Final resolution: exactly 1399 × 1124 pixels.**
- Landscape orientation, approximately 5:4.
- The artwork must completely occupy the image canvas.
- No blank margins, letterboxing, external backgrounds, or transparent padding.
- No visible tabletop or presentation surface.
- No external postcard shadows.

Compose the artwork natively for these dimensions.

Do not resize an incompatible image by stretching or squeezing it.

If an existing composition does not fit, reposition, extend, crop, or reconstruct the visual elements while preserving their natural proportions.

All essential performers, titles, and dates must fit comfortably within the image.

The card must remain visually effective when displayed on the website at approximately **254 × 204 pixels**.

---

## 3. Artistic Style

Reproduce the aesthetic of premium American radio advertising, illustrated entertainment magazines, and theatrical promotional cards from approximately 1935–1955.

### Required characteristics

- Cinematic, semi-photorealistic illustration combined with vintage commercial poster artistry.
- Authentic mid-century magazine advertising craftsmanship.
- Believable human faces with recognizable, naturally proportioned facial features.
- Rich photographic detail enhanced by restrained painterly rendering.
- Dramatic period studio lighting and carefully controlled contrast.
- Deep shadows, atmospheric depth, and natural highlights.
- Subtle halftone printing characteristics.
- Aged lithographic ink textures.
- Minor color-registration imperfections.
- Paper grain, fine scratches, light stains, and organically worn printing.
- Warm, tactile qualities associated with vintage printed materials.

The image must resemble a professionally commissioned illustration produced during the program's original broadcast era.

Avoid the appearance of contemporary digital concept art, generic AI illustration, cartoon artwork, or modern movie-poster design.

---

## 4. Composition and Principal Performers

Design each card around **one or two dominant performers or defining characters**.

### Single-performer programs

Use one clearly recognizable dominant performer when a radio program is strongly associated with a particular star.

The primary performer should occupy a substantial portion of the composition and establish the immediate visual identity of the program.

### Two-performer programs

Where two performers are strongly associated with the program, include both when historically appropriate.

Examples include:

- Married performing couples.
- Detective and assistant partnerships.
- Comedic double acts.
- Two central dramatic characters.

Balance the figures naturally without forcing symmetrical poses.

One performer may remain compositionally dominant where historically appropriate.

### Ensemble programs

Do not attempt to include the entire cast.

Select the one or two performers most recognizable or most important to the show's identity.

### Programs without a recognizable principal performer

For anthology, science-fiction, suspense, adventure, or similar productions without a central on-air character, feature one or two dramatic subjects or a defining visual scene representing the program.

### Anatomical requirements

- Preserve natural head-to-body proportions.
- Maintain recognizable facial structures.
- Use believable facial expressions.
- Ensure hands, fingers, and limbs are anatomically correct.
- Avoid unnaturally staged or exaggerated poses.
- Never compress or distort human features to satisfy the aspect ratio.

The composition must be created around the subjects, not the subjects distorted to accommodate the frame.

---

## 5. Program-Specific Environments

Construct a cinematic background associated with the program's genre, story world, historical period, or recognizable promotional imagery.

Possible treatments include:

**Comedy and variety**

Broadcast studios, theatrical stages, orchestra settings, vintage microphones, velvet curtains, studio spotlights, and period entertainment interiors.

**Detective and crime**

Shadowed detective offices, rain-soaked streets, period automobiles, city skylines, fedoras, dramatic interiors, and atmospheric cigarette smoke.

**Mystery and suspense**

Threatening shadows, mysterious interiors, dramatic lighting, isolated architecture, fog, and psychologically tense environments.

**Western**

Frontier settlements, saloons, horseback riders, plains, desert landscapes, and period Western clothing.

**Science fiction**

Retro-futuristic spacecraft, planetary landscapes, observatories, radio laboratories, strange worlds, and imaginative machinery reflecting the science-fiction illustration of the broadcast era.

**Adventure**

Distant landscapes, ships, aircraft, wilderness settings, dangerous terrain, and dramatic historical environments.

### Environmental requirements

The background must enhance the identity of the radio program without overwhelming the performers or title.

Use a single coherent composition, not a collage of unrelated scenes.

The environment should feel integrated with the principal subject through lighting, atmosphere, perspective, and color.

---

## 6. Historically Authentic, Show-Specific Typography

**The title typography must be researched and designed individually for each radio program.**

Do not apply the same lettering style indiscriminately across the collection.

### Primary references

Examine historical examples of:

- The show's original magazine advertising.
- Radio network promotional artwork.
- Original program title cards.
- Newspaper advertisements.
- Publicity posters.
- Studio promotional materials.

Use the program's original advertising typography as the principal inspiration for the new card.

### Typography treatment

Where historically appropriate, use:

- Bold dimensional display lettering.
- Period-authentic serif or sans-serif lettering.
- Hand-painted commercial lettering.
- Condensed theatrical typefaces.
- Script or decorative lettering associated with the original program.
- Carefully controlled drop shadows.
- Subtle beveling and printed highlights.
- Antique cream, ivory, gold, silver-gray, or other period-appropriate lettering colors.

Titles may be angled, arched, stacked, or arranged dynamically when supported by the original advertising identity.

### Critical typography rules

- The title must be immediately legible.
- The lettering must be a prominent element of the artwork.
- Typography must integrate naturally with the illustration.
- The title must remain within the printed area.
- Do not allow important lettering to be lost beneath distressing or edge damage.
- Avoid modern font treatments and contemporary branding conventions.
- Do not substitute generic vintage lettering when original promotional references are available.

### Mandatory text

**[RADIO SHOW TITLE]**

**[START YEAR] – [END YEAR]**

No additional slogans, network names, logos, descriptive text, episode titles, or decorative wording unless specifically requested.

All lettering and numerical dates must be accurate.

---

## 7. Broadcast Date Research and Display Rules

Display the earliest and latest verified years of the program's **original radio broadcasts**, regardless of interruptions between broadcast runs.

### Rules

1. **START YEAR** is the earliest documented year in which the program originally aired.
2. **END YEAR** is the latest documented year in which the program originally aired.
3. Include documented revivals or later original broadcast runs when they are properly recognized as continuations of the same radio program.
4. Do not limit the date range to the show's longest uninterrupted run.
5. Do not use years from syndicated reruns, rebroadcasts, modern restorations, or later television adaptations.
6. Research and verify both years before producing the card.
7. When a title was used for historically distinct productions, distinguish those productions before assigning the date range.

### Date typography

- Position the years near the bottom of the artwork.
- Use smaller lettering than the program title.
- Maintain sufficient contrast for readability.
- Apply a period-appropriate typographic treatment consistent with the title.
- Use the format **YYYY – YYYY**.

Do not invent or estimate dates when reliable historical information is available.

## 8. Individually Differentiated Color Palettes

**Every radio program must receive a distinctive color palette appropriate to its individual identity.**

The Jack Benny reference card establishes the quality standard, not a mandatory color scheme.

Do not repeatedly reuse the same red-and-gold composition.

### Palette development

Select colors based on:

- The show's genre.
- Its historical promotional materials.
- The emotional character of the program.
- Its principal performers and environment.
- The need for visual differentiation within the larger collection.

### Suggested palettes

| Program category        | Possible palette                                       |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Comedy and variety      | Burgundy, amber, theatrical gold, warm ivory           |
| Detective and crime     | Midnight blue, charcoal, smoky gray, muted amber       |
| Mystery and suspense    | Deep violet, black, antique brass, desaturated crimson |
| Western                 | Dusty ochre, saddle brown, copper, faded turquoise     |
| Science fiction         | Deep teal, muted cobalt, metallic gray, antique yellow |
| Adventure               | Forest green, weathered bronze, burnt sienna           |
| Historical drama        | Sepia, olive, aged parchment, charcoal                 |
| Horror and supernatural | Dark plum, oxidized green, near-black, faded gold      |

These palettes are illustrative rather than mandatory prescriptions.

Each program's historical artwork should influence its final color choices.

### Collection-level requirements

All cards must share:

- Comparable richness and tonal sophistication.
- Consistent illustration quality.
- Historically plausible printing characteristics.
- Comparable aging and distressed-edge treatments.

However, the cards should not appear to be recolored copies of one another.

Avoid fluorescent colors, excessive saturation, glossy gradients, synthetic digital lighting, and contemporary color effects.

## 9. Mandatory Distressed Postcard Edge Treatment

**This treatment must remain visually consistent across the entire Classic Radio Vault collection.**

Surround the illustrated image with an organically irregular, aged cream-paper perimeter resembling a heavily handled vintage promotional card.

### Required characteristics

- Slightly rounded outer corners.
- Irregular, naturally ragged printed edges.
- Small patches of missing, chipped, or flaking ink.
- Exposed cream-colored paper substrate.
- Fine scratches and occasional hairline cracks.
- Subtle staining and worn corners.
- Minor abrasions and naturally scuffed surfaces.
- Localized accumulation of darker pigment near portions of the printed perimeter.
- Uneven ink wear, avoiding mechanical repetition.

### Physical realism

The deterioration must resemble genuine aging of ink printed on old paper.

Wear should be irregular and slightly asymmetric.

Avoid perfectly repeated distress patterns, artificial brush borders, decorative vignettes, or uniformly applied edge effects.

### Canvas integration

The distressed card itself must occupy the entire image canvas.

There must be:

- No external background.
- No floating postcard effect.
- No drop shadow around the card.
- No simulated tabletop.
- No transparent area outside the paper.
- No ornate geometric picture frame.

The aged paper perimeter should remain relatively thin, preserving the visual importance of the artwork.

The border must be consistent in character across cards without being an identical repeated texture.

---

## 10. Historical and Visual Authenticity

Every visible design element must be plausible for the radio program's period and historical context.

### Performers

- Accurate likenesses based on contemporary publicity photographs.
- Historically appropriate hairstyles and wardrobe.
- Natural facial structure and expression.
- Appropriate age and recognizable appearance from the broadcast era.

### Environments and props

- Historically appropriate broadcast microphones.
- Period-correct furniture and architectural details.
- Authentic automobiles and transportation equipment.
- Plausible theater and radio-studio technology.
- Period-consistent advertising conventions.

### Visual storytelling

The composition must communicate the character of the program immediately.

A viewer should be able to recognize the show's genre and, when applicable, its most important performer without reading supplemental text.

Historical credibility takes precedence over exaggerated spectacle.

---

## 11. Composition and Readability at Website Size

The original artwork will be displayed at full resolution and as a smaller website card measuring approximately **254 × 204 pixels**.

Therefore:

- Faces must remain recognizable at thumbnail size.
- The title must remain easily readable.
- The principal subject must have a clear silhouette.
- Contrast must distinguish the lettering from the background.
- The composition must avoid unnecessary visual clutter.
- Fine background details must not compete with the main subject.
- Dates must remain legible without dominating the design.

Use strong visual hierarchy and deliberate placement of subjects, typography, and environmental details.

The artwork must feel balanced and compelling at both display sizes.

- Historically researched program identity.
- Correct program title and date range.
- One or two dominant performers, where appropriate.
- Recognizable, naturally proportioned human faces.
- Individually researched period typography.

- Authentic mid-century commercial illustration quality.

- Consistent distressed cream-paper perimeter.

## 13. Strict Prohibitions

Do not include:

- Modern flat graphic design.
- Cartoon, caricature, or comic-book rendering, unless expressly requested.
- Generic modern movie-poster compositions.
- Ornate Art Deco geometric picture frames.
- Clean contemporary rounded-card borders.
- Repetitive, artificial distress patterns.
- Unrelated decorative symbols.
- Extra titles, slogans, text, or network logos.
- Anatomically distorted faces, hands, or bodies.
- Oversized or unnaturally compressed heads.
- Awkward or physically implausible character poses.
- Collage panels or split-screen compositions.
- Modern clothing, microphones, architecture, or props inconsistent with the scene's period.
- External backgrounds or transparent canvas padding.
- Watermarks, artist signatures, or modern branding.
- Identical typography across unrelated radio programs.
- Repeated color palettes without an appropriate historical reason.
- Unverified broadcast years presented as confirmed facts.

## 14. Mandatory Production Workflow

For every requested radio program, follow this sequence.

### Step 1 — Research the program

Identify and verify:

- Official title.
- Earliest and latest original broadcast years.
- One or two principal performers, if applicable.
- Original advertising typography.
- Historical promotional imagery.
- Genre and defining visual characteristics.

### Step 2 — Establish the visual concept

Choose:

- Principal subject or subjects.
- Show-specific environment.
- Dominant color palette.
- Historically inspired title treatment.
- Composition and lighting approach.

### Step 3 — Produce the illustration

Create a coherent, cinematic vintage commercial illustration with the correct subject proportions and historical visual details.

Compose specifically for 1399 × 1124 pixels.

### Step 4 — Integrate typography

Add the correctly spelled program title and verified broadcast years.

Ensure strong legibility at both full resolution and website thumbnail size.

### Step 5 — Apply vintage print treatment

Integrate halftone texture, lithographic ink wear, fine paper grain, restrained printing imperfections, and the mandatory distressed cream-paper edge treatment.

### Step 6 — Validate the final image

Verify:

- Exact pixel dimensions.
- Title spelling.
- Historical date accuracy.
- Performer recognizability.
- Natural anatomy and proportions.
- Period authenticity.
- Distinctive palette and typography.
- Consistent distressed-edge characteristics.
- No additional text or unwanted objects.
- Full-bleed composition without external padding.

Correct defects before accepting the image as finished.
